## Designing an amazing investing experience

The buy/sell flow is the lifeblood of an investment app. We spent tons of time exploring and refining it to strike the balance between ease and control.

We ended up trying a much simpler approach which the Australian market hadn’t seen before. Simply decide how much money to invest and what to invest it in. Investing should feel as easy as saving, not like placing a trade.

One of our guiding principles was to make investing accessible by removing unnecessary complexity. Sometimes we pushed this too far. For example, we initially removed individual share prices, believing they were less relevant in a world of fractional investing. But users were used to seeing them, and we eventually brought them back.

Other simplifications worked perfectly, like hiding complex order types behind a simple dollar-based investment flow, while keeping advanced controls available for those who needed them.

## Putting your investing on autopilot

The most successful investors have a long term view and are consistent. We built a product that helps people invest on a recurring basis and follow healthy investing principles such as rebalancing and reinvestment of dividends.

## Less dopamine, more long term thinking

People check their investments often, and it’s important to be able to understand what’s happening at a glance. We wanted to balance between giving the user a dopamine hit - what other investment apps optimize for - while also showing what your investment currently are doing. We wanted the user to always have a long term focus, so we’re headlining the “all time” number over the daily number like many other investment apps.

## Understanding your performance

Diving deeper and understanding your performance over time helps you decide what to double down on, and what to change. By researching the market, we found out that a stair/step chart was one of the things users really loved from other investing apps so we decided to do something similar. The goal was to show long-term progress, rather than daily ups and downs.

## Challenges and lessons learned

Building Betashares Direct was a multi-year journey, with plenty of challenges, and not all ideas worked out. Along the way, I learned when to question existing standards, when to simplify, and when to take a step back.

Besides the product itself, another challenge was bringing the wider business along. Betashares was a finance company, and had never built a technology product. A big part of my role was working with senior leadership to mitigate risks, trade-offs, and go through the iterative nature of building great software.

My biggest learning is that simplicity isn't always about removing things. We need to know what matters to users and finding the right balance. It takes a lot of experimentation, hard decisions, and a willingness to change along the way.
